Week 14 — TRACE Transfer and Final Readiness

Stress-testing epistemic agency across unfamiliar tasks

TipThis week’s central question

Can you use TRACE when the problem is unfamiliar, ambiguous, and not designed to look like a classroom exercise?

Learning goals

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

  1. transfer TRACE to unfamiliar information and AI problems;
  2. identify which TRACE step a workflow is missing;
  3. choose an appropriate verification standard from context;
  4. distinguish confidence from evidence quality;
  5. audit your Final Project for missing provenance, probes, verification, and judgment;
  6. state a personal rule for when to use, modify, disclose, reject, or stop AI use.

No new external reading.

TRACE Lab

Session 1 — Transfer stations

Students rotate through short unfamiliar cases.

Possible case families:

  • a viral claim;
  • a ranked search result;
  • an AI product claim;
  • an LLM answer with citations;
  • an automated decision;
  • a professional AI-use scenario.

For each case, you have limited time.

Predict

What is the first tempting conclusion?

Encounter

What evidence is actually available?

Probe

What single test would most reduce uncertainty?

Explain

What can and cannot be inferred?

Decide

What action is justified now?

Verify

What independent evidence would change the decision?

Session 2 — Missing-step audit

Each group receives a flawed workflow.

Examples:

  • strong prompt, no verification;
  • many sources, no provenance;
  • verified facts, inflated causal claim;
  • clear AI disclosure, no independent work;
  • human approval, no real override authority;
  • huge context, no context control.

Identify the missing TRACE capacity.

TRACE transfer matrix

Ask whether the same method works across:

  • academic work;
  • workplace analysis;
  • public information;
  • high-stakes decisions;
  • everyday AI use.

The details change.

The epistemic discipline should remain recognisable.

Session 3 — Final Project readiness audit

Audit all three frozen tasks.

For every task confirm

  • provenance captured;
  • authentic task clearly defined;
  • AI role documented;
  • prompts/interactions preserved;
  • at least one meaningful probe;
  • independent verification planned;
  • failure/limitation evidence preserved;
  • final human judgment explicit;
  • AI-use disclosure complete.

Across all three tasks confirm

  • tasks are meaningfully different;
  • comparison criteria are common enough to compare;
  • verification cost can be discussed;
  • one genuine failure case is documented;
  • your Personal AI/LLM Use Charter is evidence-based.
