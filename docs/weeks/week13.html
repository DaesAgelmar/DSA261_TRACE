Week 13 — Professional Accountability and Design Freeze

Human oversight, automation bias, decision rights, stop conditions, and Final Project Gate 2

TipThis week’s central question

If a human is “in the loop”, what must that human actually be able and willing to do?

Learning goals

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

  1. distinguish nominal human presence from meaningful human oversight;
  2. explain automation bias;
  3. identify who has authority to accept, reject, override, or stop an AI-assisted process;
  4. define stop conditions before a high-stakes failure occurs;
  5. map accountability across a human–AI workflow;
  6. freeze a Final Project design that specifies responsibility and verification in advance.

Before class

REQUIRED READ — EU AI Act: Human Oversight

Open Article 14

For high-risk systems, Article 14 emphasises human capacity to understand limits, monitor operation, interpret outputs, avoid over-reliance, and disregard or override outputs when appropriate.

REQUIRED READ — AI Literacy

Open AI literacy guidance

AI literacy is context-sensitive.

The knowledge needed by a user depends on:

  • technical knowledge;
  • experience;
  • education/training;
  • use context;
  • people affected.

TRACE Lab

Session 1 — Human in the loop is not enough

Today’s Case

A professional receives an AI recommendation and clicks Approve.

Is this meaningful oversight?

Predict

What must the human know or be able to do before “approval” becomes meaningful?

Encounter — Oversight capabilities

A meaningful overseer may need to:

  • understand the system’s purpose;
  • understand relevant limitations;
  • recognise anomalous output;
  • interpret the recommendation;
  • seek additional evidence;
  • reject the output;
  • override the output;
  • stop the process;
  • document the decision.

Probe — Remove authority

Imagine the human can see the output but:

  • cannot inspect evidence;
  • cannot change the decision;
  • cannot stop the process.

Is the person truly overseeing the system?

Session 2 — Automation bias and responsibility

Accountability chain

Map:

Input → AI operation → Output → Human interpretation → Decision → Consequence → Review

Assign a responsible actor to every stage.

Pair Challenge — Stop conditions

For one professional use case define:

  • continue condition
  • verify-more condition
  • override condition
  • stop condition
  • escalation condition

Explain

Human oversight is not:

“A person was somewhere near the system.”

It is a set of competences + decision rights + verification opportunities + accountability.

Session 3 — Final Project Gate 2: Design Freeze

Bring your Week 12 Gate 1 feedback.

For each of the three Final Project tasks freeze:

  1. exact task;
  2. AI role;
  3. input/context boundary;
  4. prompt/interaction plan;
  5. required TRACE probe;
  6. verification source;
  7. evidence-capture method;
  8. expected failure mode;
  9. human decision;
  10. stop/override condition.

Final Project contract

After Gate 2, changes require instructor approval if they materially change:

  • the task;
  • the risk profile;
  • the evidence standard;
  • the final comparison.
