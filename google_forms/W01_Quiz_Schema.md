## Q1 — Claim vs evidence

**Type:** Multiple choice  
**Points:** 1  
**Required:** Yes

Which statement best distinguishes a **claim** from **evidence**?

A. A claim is any statement published by a reliable institution; evidence is any link attached to it.  
B. A claim is what is being asserted; evidence is information that can support, contradict, or qualify that assertion.  
C. Claims are subjective; evidence is always objective.  
D. A claim becomes evidence when many websites repeat it.

**Correct answer:** B

**Feedback:** Repetition does not transform a claim into independent evidence.

---

## Q2 — Repetition is not independence

**Type:** Multiple choice  
**Points:** 1  
**Required:** Yes

You find the same statistic on eight news websites. All eight cite the same unnamed “recent study.” What is the strongest next step?

A. Treat eight websites as eight independent confirmations.  
B. Search for the earliest article and stop there.  
C. Identify and inspect the original study, dataset, report, or institution behind the statistic.  
D. Ask an LLM whether the statistic sounds plausible.

**Correct answer:** C

**Feedback:** Multiple pages can reproduce the same unsupported source. Trace the claim toward original evidence.

## Q3 — Leave the page

**Type:** Multiple choice  
**Points:** 1  
**Required:** Yes

Which action best represents **leaving the page** when evaluating an unfamiliar source?

A. Read the site’s About page more carefully.  
B. Open new tabs and search independently for the organisation, author, claim, and external assessments.  
C. Count how many images the page contains.  
D. Ask the site’s chatbot whether the site is reliable.

**Correct answer:** B

**Feedback:** Independent investigation reduces reliance on a source’s self-description.

## Q4 — Verifiability

**Type:** Multiple choice  
**Points:** 1  
**Required:** Yes

Which claim is most clearly verifiable using publicly inspectable evidence?

A. The minister secretly dislikes this policy.  
B. Everyone at the meeting felt uncomfortable.  
C. This video was recorded in Ankara on 10 September 2026.  
D. The author wrote the article with bad intentions.

**Correct answer:** C

**Feedback:** Date/location/media claims can leave inspectable traces; private intentions usually cannot.

## Q5 — LLM citations

**Type:** Multiple choice  
**Points:** 1  
**Required:** Yes

An LLM gives you a confident answer and cites three sources. How should those citations be treated **before you independently check them**?

A. Verified evidence because the model provided citations.  
B. Potential leads that still require external verification.  
C. Primary sources by definition.  
D. Reliable if the titles look academic.

**Correct answer:** B

**Feedback:** LLM citations are leads until externally verified.
